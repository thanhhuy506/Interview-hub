1. Interview Strategy

This round is likely not a pure technical interview. You have already passed the technical and culture-fit rounds, so the General Director may mainly evaluate:

  • Executive-level maturity
  • Leadership capability
  • Commitment and long-term motivation
  • Japanese working-culture fit
  • Business mindset and ownership
  • Whether your value can justify a compensation exception
Core message to communicate:
A strong hands-on Java engineer with leadership experience, proven experience working with Japanese organizations, and the potential to take broader technical ownership at Collected Mock.

2. Tell Me About Yourself

Question

Please introduce yourself.

Recommended Answer

Thank you for giving me the opportunity to meet with you.

I am a Java engineer with around nine years of experience in software development, and currently I am working as a Java Team Leader.

My main strength is backend development, especially Java. Over the years, I have worked on system design, implementation, code review, production deployment, performance improvement and troubleshooting critical production issues.

As a team leader, my responsibility is not only to develop software, but also to understand requirements, design solutions, break down tasks, support team members and make sure the team delivers with good quality.

Another important part of my background is that I have worked with Japanese companies for around seven years. Because of that, I am familiar with Japanese working culture, especially the importance of quality, responsibility, communication and commitment.

At this stage of my career, I am looking for an environment where I can contribute technically, take more ownership and continue developing myself as both an engineer and a leader.

That is one of the main reasons I am very interested in Collected Mock.

3. Why Collected Mock?

Question

Why do you want to join Collected Mock?

Recommended Answer

There are three main reasons.

First, Collected Mockis a large technology-driven company where engineering has a direct impact on the business. I want to work on systems where scalability, reliability and quality really matter.

Second, I am interested in Collected Mock's international working environment. I have already worked with Japanese companies for many years, but I would like to work in an even more global environment and collaborate with people from different countries and backgrounds.

Third, I like Collected Mock's culture of continuous improvement and getting things done. At this stage of my career, I don't want to only complete assigned development tasks. I want to take ownership, solve bigger problems and contribute to both the team and the organization.

4. Why Should We Hire You?

Question

Why should we hire you?

Recommended Answer

I believe I can bring value in three areas.

First is technical capability. I have around nine years of software development experience with a strong focus on Java backend systems, and I can work independently on complex technical problems.

Second is leadership. I am currently leading a Java team, so I am comfortable with technical design, code review, mentoring developers, task planning and supporting the team when we have difficult production issues.

Third is my experience working with Japanese organizations. After working in Japanese companies for around seven years, I understand the importance of commitment, quality, transparency and communication.

So I believe I can contribute not only as an individual developer, but also as someone who can improve the overall performance of the team.
Key sentence: I can contribute not only as an individual developer, but also improve the overall performance of the team.

5. Salary Expectation – Why 75M?

Question

Your expected salary is higher than our normal package. Why do you expect 75 million?

Recommended Answer

I understand that my expectation may be higher than the standard range for the position.

My expectation is based on the overall value and responsibilities I can bring rather than only the number of years of experience.

I currently have around nine years of software development experience, including team leadership, system design, production support, code review and mentoring.

I believe I can contribute beyond the normal scope of an individual Senior Developer and gradually take more ownership within the organization.

At the same time, compensation is not the only factor for me. The role, technical challenges, working environment and long-term growth opportunities are also very important.

If we both believe there is a strong fit, I am open to having a reasonable discussion about the overall package.

6. If We Cannot Offer 75M

Question

If we cannot offer 75 million, what will you do?

Recommended Answer

Seventy-five million is my current expectation based on my experience and responsibilities.

However, I don't evaluate an opportunity based only on one number.

I would also consider the role, responsibilities, learning opportunities, career path, benefits and the overall package.

Collected Mockis an opportunity that I am seriously interested in, so I would prefer to understand the complete offer before making a final decision.
Avoid: saying “75 million is negotiable” immediately. It weakens your position unnecessarily.

7. Why Are You Leaving Your Current Company?

Question

Why are you leaving your current company?

Recommended Answer

I have had a good experience with my current company and I appreciate what I have learned there.

My reason for considering a change is mainly career growth.

After around nine years in software development, I want to challenge myself in a larger and more international organization where I can work on larger-scale systems and continue developing my technical and leadership skills.

I am not leaving because of a negative relationship with my current company. I simply feel that this is the right time for my next challenge.
Optional line: I am not running away from something. I am moving toward my next challenge.

8. Japanese Working Culture

Question

You have worked for Japanese companies for seven years. What have you learned from Japanese working culture?

Recommended Answer

One important thing I have learned is responsibility.

In my experience working with Japanese teams, when we commit to something, we should take responsibility for delivering it and communicate early if there is any risk.

I also learned the importance of preparation and quality. Before implementation, we should understand the requirements carefully and consider possible risks instead of fixing everything after release.

At the same time, I learned that communication is extremely important, especially when Vietnamese and Japanese members may have different expectations or communication styles.

So I try to communicate clearly, confirm important decisions and avoid assumptions.

These experiences have helped me become more disciplined and reliable as both an engineer and a team leader.
Keywords: Responsibility — Preparation — Quality — Clear communication — No assumptions

9. Leadership Style

Question

What is your leadership style?

Recommended Answer

My leadership style is supportive but accountable.

I don't want team members to depend on me for every decision. I try to give them enough context and ownership so they can work independently.

At the same time, I make sure expectations, priorities and responsibilities are clear.

When someone has difficulty, I first try to understand the root cause and support them through discussion, technical guidance or mentoring.

My goal is not to become the strongest person in the team. My goal is to make the whole team stronger.
Memorize this: My goal is not to become the strongest person in the team. My goal is to make the whole team stronger.

10. Managing an Underperforming Developer

Question

How do you manage a developer who is not performing well?

Recommended Answer

First, I would avoid making a conclusion too quickly.

I would have a one-on-one discussion to understand the reason. The problem could be technical knowledge, unclear requirements, workload, communication or something else.

Then I would define clear expectations and a concrete improvement plan.

For example, I might provide mentoring, assign smaller milestones or pair the developer with a stronger team member.

After that, I would monitor progress and give frequent feedback.

My first goal is always to help the person improve. But at the same time, as a team leader, I also have responsibility for the team's delivery, so if there is no improvement after reasonable support, I would discuss the next action with my manager.

11. Disagreement With Your Manager

Question

How do you handle disagreement with your manager?

Recommended Answer

I believe disagreement itself is not a problem. The important thing is how we handle it.

If I disagree with my manager, I will first try to understand the reason behind the decision.

If I still have concerns, I will explain my opinion with facts, technical evidence and possible business impact rather than simply saying that I disagree.

Once a final decision is made, unless there is a serious security, compliance or ethical concern, I will support the decision and help the team execute it successfully.

I think a professional can disagree during the discussion but still commit after the decision.
Key idea: Disagree during discussion, commit after the decision.

12. Difficult Situation as a Leader

Question

Tell me about a difficult situation you handled as a leader.

Recommended Answer

One example was a critical production issue that affected an important business function.

My first priority was to stabilize the situation rather than immediately finding someone to blame.

I divided the team into different responsibilities. Some members investigated logs and application behavior, while others checked recent releases and database activity.

At the same time, I kept stakeholders updated about our progress and risks.

After identifying the root cause, we implemented the safest solution, verified it carefully and released the fix.

After the incident, we conducted a root-cause analysis and added preventive measures such as better monitoring and additional test cases.

What I learned from that experience is that during a critical situation, technical skill is important, but communication, prioritization and calm decision-making are equally important.

13. Risk of Missing a Deadline

Question

What do you do when your team may miss a deadline?

Recommended Answer

I try to identify the risk as early as possible.

First, I check the remaining work and identify the real bottleneck.

Then I look at possible options: reducing non-critical scope, reallocating resources, simplifying the solution or adjusting priorities.

Most importantly, I communicate the risk early to stakeholders.

I don't believe in hiding a problem until the deadline.

If the deadline is fixed, I work with the team to find the best solution while protecting the critical quality of the product.
Strong line: I believe risks should be communicated early, not when they become problems.

14. Speed vs Quality

Question

Speed or quality—which is more important?

Recommended Answer

I don't think speed and quality should always be treated as opposites.

The important thing is understanding which quality attributes are non-negotiable.

For example, in a financial system, security, data correctness and transaction integrity cannot be compromised.

However, we can still improve delivery speed by reducing unnecessary complexity, automating testing and deployment, breaking work into smaller releases and making decisions faster.

For me, good engineering means delivering the right level of quality as quickly as possible.

15. Technical Excellence vs Business Requirements

Question

How do you balance technical excellence and business requirements?

Recommended Answer

As engineers, it is easy to focus only on building the technically perfect solution.

But the best technical solution is not always the best business solution.

Before making a design decision, I try to understand the business goal, expected scale, timeline, risks and future direction.

Then I choose a solution that is simple enough for the current requirement but flexible enough for reasonable future changes.

I try to avoid both under-engineering and over-engineering.
Strong line: Technology should support the business, not become the goal itself.

16. Tell Me About a Failure

Question

Tell me about a failure or mistake.

Recommended Answer

Earlier in my career, I sometimes focused too much on solving technical problems myself.

When I became a leader, I realized that this approach did not scale. If every difficult problem depended on me, I became a bottleneck and team members had fewer opportunities to grow.

So I changed my approach.

Instead of immediately giving the solution, I started asking questions, sharing the context and helping team members find the solution themselves.

As a result, team members became more independent and I had more time to focus on architecture and higher-level problems.

That experience changed my understanding of leadership.

17. Biggest Weakness

Question

What is your biggest weakness?

Recommended Answer

One area I have been improving is delegation.

Because I have a strong technical background, in the past I sometimes preferred to handle difficult technical tasks myself because I believed it would be faster.

However, as my leadership responsibilities increased, I realized that this approach can create a dependency on the leader.

Now I delegate more intentionally, provide guidance and allow team members to own the solution.

This has helped both the team and myself grow.

18. Developing Team Members

Question

How do you develop your team members?

Recommended Answer

I believe development should be different for each person.

Some developers need deeper technical knowledge, while others need opportunities in communication, design or leadership.

I normally observe their strengths and areas for improvement, discuss their goals during one-on-one conversations and then give them suitable challenges.

For example, instead of always asking a senior developer to design a solution, I may let another member prepare the first proposal and then review it together.

I also encourage knowledge sharing and code review within the team.

My goal is to gradually make team members able to handle more responsibility independently.

19. Working Across Countries

Question

How do you work with teams from different countries?

Recommended Answer

I think the biggest challenge in cross-country collaboration is often not technical knowledge but differences in context and communication styles.

My approach is to make communication explicit.

I clearly document important requirements and decisions, confirm understanding after discussions and avoid assuming that everyone interprets something in the same way.

When there is disagreement, I focus the discussion on facts, requirements and business goals rather than personal opinions.

Working with Japanese teams for many years has taught me to be especially careful about this.

I believe strong cross-country collaboration requires clarity, respect and frequent communication.

20. Technical Decision-Making

Question

How do you make technical decisions?

Recommended Answer

I normally start from the problem rather than the technology.

First, I clarify the requirements, constraints and expected scale.

Then I compare possible solutions based on factors such as maintainability, performance, reliability, development cost and operational complexity.

For important decisions, I discuss the options with the team because different perspectives often reveal risks that one person may miss.

Finally, I document important decisions so that future team members understand not only what we decided, but why we decided it.

21. Disagreeing With an Existing Collected MockApproach

Question

What would you do if you joined Collected Mockand discovered something you disagreed with?

Recommended Answer

First, I would spend time understanding the context.

A system or process that looks inefficient from the outside may exist for reasons that I don't yet understand.

After understanding the background, if I still see an opportunity for improvement, I would prepare a proposal with the expected benefits, risks and implementation approach.

I prefer to improve things through evidence and collaboration rather than simply criticizing the existing way of working.

26. Customer Satisfaction for an Engineer

Question

What does customer satisfaction mean to an engineer?

Recommended Answer

Engineers may not always communicate directly with customers, but almost every engineering decision affects the customer experience.

Performance affects how quickly customers complete an action.

Reliability affects whether they trust the service.

Security protects their information and money.

Even maintainability eventually affects how quickly we can deliver improvements.

So I believe customer satisfaction should also be part of engineering decision-making, not only a responsibility of the business team.

27. What Does Ownership Mean?

Question

What does ownership mean to you?

Recommended Answer

For me, ownership means not stopping at “my code is finished.”

I should care whether the requirement is correct, whether the solution works in production, whether the team can maintain it and whether the customer receives the expected value.

If a problem happens, my first question should be “How can we solve it?” rather than “Whose fault is it?”

That is how I try to work as both an engineer and a leader.

28. Wrong Decision as a Leader

Question

What if you make a wrong decision as a leader?

Recommended Answer

Leaders will not always make perfect decisions.

If I realize that I made a wrong decision, I think the most important thing is to acknowledge it quickly rather than defend it because of ego.

I would evaluate the impact, communicate transparently with the team and stakeholders, and correct the direction as quickly as possible.

After that, I would understand why the decision was wrong and improve the decision-making process.

For me, leadership is not about always being right. It is about taking responsibility for the result.

I enjoy solving difficult problems, but what motivates me even more now is seeing a team become stronger and seeing a system become better because of decisions we made.

I also enjoy learning. Even after nine years in software development, there are still many areas I want to understand more deeply.

That continuous improvement is one of the reasons I still enjoy engineering.

34. What Does Professionalism Mean?

Question

What does professionalism mean to you?

Recommended Answer

For me, professionalism means being someone that the team can rely on.

It means preparing carefully, keeping commitments, communicating problems early, continuously improving my skills and taking responsibility for the result.

A professional does not only perform well when everything is going smoothly. The real test is how we behave when there is pressure or when something goes wrong.

35. Working Under Pressure

Question

Are you comfortable working under pressure?

Recommended Answer

Yes. I have worked on production systems and have handled critical issues where decisions had to be made quickly.

Under pressure, I try to separate urgency from panic.

I identify the highest priority, collect the necessary information, assign clear responsibilities and keep stakeholders informed.

I have learned that staying calm helps the entire team perform better.
Strong phrase: Separate urgency from panic.

36. Questions to Ask the General Director

At the end of the interview, ask one or two executive-level questions.

  1. From your perspective, what distinguishes engineers who become highly successful at Collected Mockfrom engineers who are simply good technically?
  2. What are the biggest engineering or organizational challenges that you expect Collected MockFintech Vietnam to solve over the next few years?
  3. For someone joining at a Senior or technical leadership level, what would you expect them to achieve within the first six to twelve months?
  4. How do you see the role of Collected MockFintech Vietnam evolving within the wider Collected Mockorganization?
Best combination: Question 1 + Question 3.
